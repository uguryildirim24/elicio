# Fit range study — right-ear behind-the-ear shell

**Rolf: if your ear *attachment* is as long as the median Korean adult man's, the current 47.90 mm shell clears its 50.9 mm length gate; so do men at the 75th and 90th percentiles. Proceeding without measuring M1 is reasonable for a reversible, no-electronics fit print, not for claiming a confirmed fit or ordering a final assembly. Your description “big ears” may mean outer-ear height, not attachment length. Keep the small 18 mm-wide, 8.1 mm-thick, 40 mAh body for the first fit check; don't redesign it into a thicker device just to accommodate small ears you have not asked it to fit.**

Study date: 2026-09-25. Public literature only. No board, shell, plan or firmware changed. This is a design envelope, not an order recommendation or a promise of universal fit. The term *M1* here means the **straight** distance from otobasion superius (upper ear-to-head attachment) to otobasion inferius (lower attachment), not the outermost ear height or the along-crease arc. Anatomical lower landmark depends on lobe attachment. All quoted percentages below assume a normal distribution *within each published sample*; none is a measured fit rate. None of the sources publishes an M1 percentile or raw M1 histogram; calculated tails are not published percentiles.

## First answer: will this fit a big adult ear?

The gate is **M1 ≥ 47.90 + 3 = 50.90 mm**. In the directly measured Korean adult male right-ear distribution [K] (n=331, mean 54.2, SD 10.0 mm), the **modeled** 50th, 75th and 90th-percentile M1 values are respectively **54.2, 60.94 and 67.02 mm** (mean + 0, 0.674, 1.282 SD). Each clears by **3.3, 10.04 and 16.12 mm**. Conditional on a man's *M1* being at or above any of these modeled cutoffs, **100% clear this one-sided length gate by definition**. That does not mean 100% physically fit: the hook and three contact sites may still miss or pinch. The sampled population overall is only ~63% over the gate. These percentiles are normal-model estimates, not reported empirical percentiles.

Cross-check, not adult-specific: the north-Indian male **mixed-age** M1 distribution [I] (mean 50.25, SD 5.28; includes some under-18s) gives modeled median 50.25, 75th 53.81 and 90th 57.02 mm. At or above its 75th/90th percentile the gate always clears; among the *upper half* from its median it clears in roughly **90%**, because the gate sits 0.65 mm above that median. These two samples cannot give a probability that *Rolf*, whose M1 is unmeasured, clears it. “Big ears” only raises confidence if it means long **attachments**, not just tall pinnae. An inexpensive passive print followed by the wear and three-contact checks is the reasonable no-measurement next step; don't pay for final assembled hardware on this inference alone.

## Cheapest downward fit gains without a bigger body

1. **Keep v4 dimensions, print and try it first.** Chord 47.90 × width 18 × thickness 8.1 mm; 40 mAh, ~5.7 h *estimated*, no board redesign. v4's routed board still needs its own proposed shell changes (`board-v4-design.md` §7); the old reference M1=52 was not Rolf's measurement. A passive fit print checks whether any downward change is necessary at all.
2. **Try one identical electronics cavity with small/medium/large *hook/contour* prints.** Keep the 47.90 chord, 18 width, 8.1 thickness, 40 mAh, ~5.7 h. Only shell CAD/prints and fit testing, if contact and hook placement remain valid; this may improve retention across shapes without making the case wider or thicker. A genuinely shorter S *chord* is **not** possible by trimming around the unchanged board and reference contact at s=43; even the S print retains the M1≥50.9 gate. The variant label is not a fit guarantee, and Rolf need not choose a size by measuring first: use non-electrical try-ons. Changes in hook size can themselves add protrusion outside the 18 × 8.1 body envelope; do not claim a finished outer size until CAD verifies the whole part.
3. **Investigate zero spare length as a trial screen, not a certified new gate.** A 47.90 mm chord with M1≥47.90 instead of ≥50.90 predicts ~74% rather than ~63% in Korean adult men; unchanged 18 × 8.1 mm and 40 mAh. The +3 was a conservative placement allowance, not an experimentally derived tissue/impedance threshold (§gate below). Zero margin risks tail/lobe pressure and lost reference contact; accept it *only* on a print that passes movement, four-hour comfort and all three paper-strip/contact checks. Do not remove the release CAD gate by assumption.
4. **If short by only ~1.5 mm, consider the documented 401010 cell N4.** It targets chord 46.40 × 18 × ~7.6 mm with a **30 mAh** cell (~4.3 h), the same main electronics island but a re-cut flex plate and revised shell. Gate 49.4 predicts ~68% Korean adult male pass versus ~63%; this costs ~1.4 h modeled runtime and is **not** a simple print-only variant. Battery listing $10 versus baseline ~$3.50–5.00 before fitting/shipping (`board-v4-design.md` §1.1). Don't pay this cost until the print shows a need.

None of these inexpensive steps certifies nearly all adults; they prioritize Rolf's stated large ear and small body. Charging remains off-ear. Reference numbers for genuinely small-ear redesigns follow the population and gate evidence below.

## What the 3 mm gate actually does

`docs/fab/plan.md` §3.2–3.3 builds a 48.4 mm **curved arc** from upper hook root to lower tail; at the default 3 mm bow its *straight chord* is 47.9005 mm. The shell frame rotates the whole body to put the tail tip 1.5 mm medial to the skull plane (preload); contacts project medially from the face. The CAD check in `scripts/cad/bte_fit_shell.py` (`M1_gate`) rejects M1 < TOTAL_CHORD + **3.0**, and `packing-v2.md` §6 reports 50.9005 against an **unmeasured default** M1=52. `plan-v2.md` §4 repeats this as a geometry/order gate, not as a measured human fit threshold. In particular, the code has no mesh of the pinna or skull and does not collide the tail with an individual lobe. No cited plan, drawing or code derives 3 mm from tissue compression, manufacturing tolerance or electrode impedance. It is a conservative **length/placement allowance** (body shorter than root by 3 mm), not a validated safety factor. Bow, preload, hook thickness, sulcus depth and lobe shape remain independent.

If M1 < 50.9 but ≥47.9, the body may physically fit between attachment landmarks, with less than 3 mm of spare length. That is a *relaxed geometric screen* **M1 ≥ chord**, not an approved comfortable/electrically reliable fit. If M1 <47.9 and the hook still hangs at the upper root, the tail extends below the lower root by approximately chord−M1 (projection/rotation change the exact amount): it may land harmlessly on exposed skin, press the lobe root or jaw, lever the hook out, or change preload so a contact floats. No anatomy model distinguishes those outcomes. The old reference contact sits at arc s=43 and can fall below the ear attachment on a short ear; both SIG rings at s≈22,33 and REF at s=43 (plan §3.3) must still press **mastoid skin**. The length gate directly guards *fit/location*, indirectly guards *comfort* and *contact quality* by avoiding the tail/lobe collision and lever arm. It does not itself test skin pressure, impedance, stable electrode contact, or RF range. `plan.md` §3.7 specifies wear/paper-strip tests; these, not the arithmetic, can determine those outcomes. The v4 board note §1.4 changes width/thickness, **not** chord or electrode sites.

## Predicted length-screen coverage

With Φ the standard-normal CDF, `pass = Φ((mean − gate)/SD)`. Use **right-ear** values: the design is right-handed; never double the sample by counting both ears. Rounded percentage of *each source's sampled distribution* (not the world's adults):

| Korean **adult** men (n=331) | 63% | 74% | 68% | 99.1% |
| Korean **adult** women (n=300) | 63% | 76% | 70% | 99.7% |

The last two rows use published age-stratum means/SDs in [I], not its male/female rows. They cannot establish adult **sex-specific** coverage; the mixed-age rows cannot establish adult-only coverage. The Korean 20–29 group alone has strict predicted male ~47%, female ~43% (n=50 each), versus all-age ~63%; older ears inflate the aggregate. Sampling, landmark placement, heavy tails and fit geometry can each dominate the nominal point percentages. Even a *strict gate pass* does not mean the earpiece fits behind the ear; no observed on-ear success rate exists. The permissive screen is offered to show sensitivity to the unvalidated 3 mm margin, **not** to waive it.

## Recommendation and first physical proof

**Proceed with a passive print of the compact v4 envelope first**, using the 47.90 chord, 18 width, 8.1 thickness and 40 mAh design; do not claim it is confirmed to fit or order final assembled hardware without a fit check. A man at or above the modeled median Korean adult male *attachment length* passes the 50.9 gate, but Rolf's M1 remains unknown. Try unchanged-cavity hook/contour variants if the first print feels wrong. Only consider the thinner 401010 (~46.4 chord, ~7.6 thick, 30 mAh) if a modest length deficit appears; it costs ~1.4 hours modeled runtime and board-flex/shell work. Do not recommend the 12–14 mm stacked redesign to someone who says he has big ears and wants a small body. No print-only S/M/L series can make the unchanged 47.90 mm electronics body pass a 30.5 mm M1 gate.

Print a **non-electrical fit gauge** at the current dimensions with dummy domes and the flat 1 mm snap-latch lid. Secure representative mass in the cavity (the earlier `plan.md` §3.7 uses about 4 g) so the retention check is not on an unrealistically light empty shell. Run that section's removal, head movement, glasses and progressive-wear/paper-strip checks on Rolf's ear; stop on pain or lasting redness. Its tape fallback is for the old closure, not approval of the v4 snap: print and cycle the new latch separately. The print must show that the hook sits, lower end does not press the lobe, and all three dummy tips retain skin contact through movement. `plan-v2.md` S0 still requires a measured M1 and template; the print does not silently waive that release gate. Resolve the no-measurement path explicitly before any board or final assembly order. An assembled, safely powered and off-charge device must later demonstrate stable contact impedance and useful EEG; neither a CT distribution nor a rendered shell proves that. Only if future users require near-universal fit should a separately funded small-ear board and mastoid-contact redesign be studied.
