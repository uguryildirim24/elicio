# Fit range study — right-ear behind-the-ear shell

**Rolf: the current 47.90 mm body passes its length rule for about 63% of Korean adult women and men, but only about 10% of women and 45% of men in a second, mixed-age north-Indian sample. Those are *predicted geometric gate passes*, not demonstrated comfortable fits or a global population share. A genuinely small-ear design would need roughly a 27.5 mm chord and a new board/contact architecture; retaining a 30 mAh cell might mean ~12 mm thickness instead of 8.1 mm and ~4.3 instead of ~5.7 estimated hours. No existing board or printable shell is proven to meet that target.**

Study date: 2026-09-25. Public literature only. No board, shell, plan or firmware changed. This is a design envelope, not an order recommendation or a promise of universal fit. The term *M1* here means the **straight** distance from otobasion superius (upper ear-to-head attachment) to otobasion inferius (lower attachment), not the outermost ear height or the along-crease arc. Anatomical lower landmark depends on lobe attachment. All quoted percentages below assume a normal distribution *within each published sample*; none is a measured fit rate. None of the sources publishes an M1 percentile or raw M1 histogram; calculated tails are not published percentiles.

Without adult-only sex strata for [I] or replicated direct M1 distributions across populations, a defensible worldwide 99% claim is impossible.

## What the 3 mm gate actually does

`docs/fab/plan.md` §3.2–3.3 builds a 48.4 mm **curved arc** from upper hook root to lower tail; at the default 3 mm bow its *straight chord* is 47.9005 mm. The shell frame rotates the whole body to put the tail tip 1.5 mm medial to the skull plane (preload); contacts project medially from the face. The CAD check in `scripts/cad/bte_fit_shell.py` (`M1_gate`) rejects M1 < TOTAL_CHORD + **3.0**, and `packing-v2.md` §6 reports 50.9005 against an **unmeasured default** M1=52. `plan-v2.md` §4 repeats this as a geometry/order gate, not as a measured human fit threshold. In particular, the code has no mesh of the pinna or skull and does not collide the tail with an individual lobe. No cited plan, drawing or code derives 3 mm from tissue compression, manufacturing tolerance or electrode impedance. It is a conservative **length/placement allowance** (body shorter than root by 3 mm), not a validated safety factor. Bow, preload, hook thickness, sulcus depth and lobe shape remain independent.

If M1 < 50.9 but ≥47.9, the body may physically fit between attachment landmarks, with less than 3 mm of spare length. That is a *relaxed geometric screen* **M1 ≥ chord**, not an approved comfortable/electrically reliable fit. If M1 <47.9 and the hook still hangs at the upper root, the tail extends below the lower root by approximately chord−M1 (projection/rotation change the exact amount): it may land harmlessly on exposed skin, press the lobe root or jaw, lever the hook out, or change preload so a contact floats. No anatomy model distinguishes those outcomes. The old reference contact sits at arc s=43 and can fall below the ear attachment on a short ear; both SIG rings at s≈22,33 and REF at s=43 (plan §3.3) must still press **mastoid skin**. The length gate directly guards *fit/location*, indirectly guards *comfort* and *contact quality* by avoiding the tail/lobe collision and lever arm. It does not itself test skin pressure, impedance, stable electrode contact, or RF range. `plan.md` §3.7 specifies wear/paper-strip tests; these, not the arithmetic, can determine those outcomes. The v4 board note §1.4 changes width/thickness, **not** chord or electrode sites.

## Predicted length-screen coverage

With Φ the standard-normal CDF, `pass = Φ((mean − gate)/SD)`. Use **right-ear** values: the design is right-handed; never double the sample by counting both ears. Rounded percentage of *each source's sampled distribution* (not the world's adults):

| Sample | Strict M1≥50.90 (47.90 chord + 3) | Length-only M1≥47.90 (zero allowance) | N4 401010 cell: M1≥49.40 (46.40+3) | Proposed chord 27.5: M1≥30.5 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Korean **adult** men (n=331) | 63% | 74% | 68% | 99.1% |
| Korean **adult** women (n=300) | 63% | 76% | 70% | 99.7% |
| North Indian **mixed-age** males (n=222) | 45% | 67% | 56% | ~100% |
| North Indian **mixed-age** females (n=178) | 10% | 23% | 16% | 99.5% |
| North Indian 26–40 y, sexes pooled (n=155) | 23% | 42% | 32% | 99.8% |
| North Indian >40 y, sexes pooled (n=124) | 51% | 70% | 61% | >99.9% |

The last two rows use published age-stratum means/SDs in [I], not its male/female rows. They cannot establish adult **sex-specific** coverage; the mixed-age rows cannot establish adult-only coverage. The Korean 20–29 group alone has strict predicted male ~47%, female ~43% (n=50 each), versus all-age ~63%; older ears inflate the aggregate. Sampling, landmark placement, heavy tails and fit geometry can each dominate the nominal point percentages. Even a *strict gate pass* does not mean the earpiece fits behind the ear; no observed on-ear success rate exists. The permissive screen is offered to show sensitivity to the unvalidated 3 mm margin, **not** to waive it.

Target for comparing concepts: nominal **≥99% length-screen pass per sex in the Korean adult and the mixed-age Indian samples**, under the existing +3 rule. The worst of the four modeled 1st percentiles is Korean men at 30.94 mm; choose a round **30.5 mm gate / 27.5 mm chord** (99.1% in that sample). This does **not** demonstrate 99% worldwide or even 99% adult Indian men/women: [I] lacks age-by-sex cells. The upper 99th-percentile man does not create an upper limit on *this* one-sided rule; a fixed hook still needs larger-ear tests. A physical sample could fail at any M1. ## Recommendation and first physical proof

**Do not order the existing 47.9 mm shell as a universal fit, and do not call N4 or three print sizes a fix for short ears.** If the aim is nearly every adult without measuring Rolf, commission a *paper/CAD feasibility design* for option D: 27.5 mm chord, target 18–22 mm wide and 12–14 mm thick with 30 mAh (~4.3 h modeled), hook at upper root, three titanium tips on the mastoid skin, off-ear charging, flat 1 mm snap-latch lid. This is ~20.4 mm shorter and possibly 3.9–5.9 mm thicker than v4, with 10 mAh and ~1.4 h less unverified runtime. If 40 mAh can be physically stacked without compromising contacts and RF, keep it; do not promise it. A retained 18 mm width is a goal; 22 mm would give back the width Rolf wanted removed. Prioritize skin contact and RF over forcing the old board inside an impossible shell. The +3 rule is a *screen*, not the empirical proof; a genuinely all-adult promise remains unsupported by these data.

Before layout/reprint, establish cross-sectional space, component-courtyard and RF clearance, whether separate tips can land at three real mastoid sites across small and large ears, no pinna/lobe pinch, mechanical retention with glasses, no skin overheating/charging on-ear, and the 40/30 mAh electrical/runtime budget. Then print **non-electrical fit gauges** at 27.5 and intermediate lengths with dummy domes and run plan §3.7's removal, head movement, glasses and progressive-wear/contact-paper tests on real consenting adult ears spanning short and long M1, different lobe attachments, and both sexes; stop on pain/redness. Only a real print and measured contact impedance/motion on an assembled, safely powered unit can demonstrate stable EEG contact and comfortable wear. This study cannot infer those from CT, gate arithmetic or a rendered shell.
